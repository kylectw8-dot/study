Unit 1

Urban land uses

1.1 What are urban areas?

Where are they? What are they like?

Urban areas 市區 = places that have been developed. Also called built-up areas 已建設地區.

Rural areas 郊區 = places that are not much developed.

Where are urban areas?

  • The northern part of Hong Kong Island
  • Kowloon
  • New towns in the New Territories (e.g. Tsuen Wan, Sha Tin, Tuen Mun)

Where are rural areas? All over the New Territories and the outlying islands 離島.

What are urban areas like?

  • 🏢 Tall and dense buildings
  • 👥 High population density (many people)
  • 💼 Intense business activities
  • 🏥 Enough community facilities (schools, hospitals)
  • 🚇 Good road and transport networks
Urban = busy. Many buildings, many people, many shops, many roads.
On the map: Hong Kong Island is at the bottom, Kowloon is just above it, and the New Territories is the big part at the top.

1.2 The 7 major types of land use

What do we use the land for?

Land use 土地用途 = the way we use a piece of land.

TypeWhat it is forExamples
ResidentialPeople live hereHousing estates, flats
CommercialPeople buy, sell, do businessShopping centre, offices, banks
IndustrialPeople make thingsFactory, industrial estate
InstitutionalGovernment and community servicesHospital, school, government offices
RecreationalPeople relax and playPark, sports ground, country park
TransportPeople and goods moveAirport, roads, railway, port
Mixed
商住混合		One building, more than one useShops downstairs, flats upstairs
Hospital = Institutional (not commercial). Airport = Transport.

Mixed land use (commercial–residential)

  • ⬆️ Upper floors: people live → residential use
  • ⬇️ Lower floors: shops and business → commercial use
  • Buildings have more than one use
  • Common in old urban areas (e.g. Mong Kok, Sham Shui Po). Not typical of new towns.
7 types in order: R C I I R T M → "Really Cool Iguanas Investigate Rocky Trails Maybe"

1.3 Where are the land uses found?

Which is biggest? Where is each one?

  • Residential is the largest type of urban land use. It is widely distributed (found all over Hong Kong).
  • Transport is the next largest. Transport land is found all over Hong Kong.
  • Commercial is small in area. It is concentrated in the city centre (Central, Admiralty, Wan Chai, Tsim Sha Tsui, Mong Kok).
  • Industrial is found in old urban areas (e.g. Kwun Tong) and new towns (e.g. Tai Po). Not in the city centre.
  • Institutional: e.g. Government Headquarters in Admiralty.
  • Recreational: e.g. Sai Kung (country parks, beaches).
Commercial land is not scattered all over. It is packed into the city centre. Residential and transport land are the ones spread all over.
Chek Lap Kok = the airport = Transport. It is NOT institutional.

Residential and industrial land uses

2.1 Types of residential areas

Where do people live? Rich, middle and poor areas.

There are 3 types of residential area: high-income 高收入, middle-income 中收入, low-income 低收入.

High-income 💰💰💰Middle-income 💰💰Low-income 💰
FlatsLarge, very expensiveMediumSmall
How crowded?Low densityHigher than high-incomeHigh density
LocationOn high ground, hillsides, by the sea, nice viewsPrivate housing estatesOld urban areas, public housing estates, squatter areas on the urban fringe
ExamplesThe Peak, Repulse Bay, Kowloon TongTaikoo Shing, Mei Foo Sun ChuenSham Shui Po, public housing estates (e.g. Kwong Fuk Estate)

2.2 How industry changed over time

Three stages. Learn them in order.

  1. ① Before the mid-1970s Factories in multi-storey factory buildings 多層工廠大廈, in old urban areas.
    Example: Kwun Tong, San Po Kong · Photo: B (old tall factory block)
  2. ② Mid-1970s to the 1990s New towns and industrial estates 工業邨 gave larger factory space.
    Example: Tai Po Industrial Estate, Yuen Long Industrial Estate · Photo: C (low, wide factory)
  3. ③ Since the late 1990s Focus on high-tech industries 高科技工業.
    Example: Hong Kong Science Park, Cyberport · Photo: A (modern building with the round "egg")
Old → tall factory blocks. Middle → big estates in new towns. Now → high-tech parks.

2.3 Are factories near homes?

The spatial relationship 空間關係 between industrial and residential areas.

Industrial areaNear homes?Why
Old industrial areas (multi-storey factory buildings)✅ Close to low-income residential areasBuilt long ago, mixed together
Industrial areas in new towns (industrial estates)↔️ Far away from residential areasPlanned. Separated by a buffer such as a park or main road
High-tech industrial areas✅ Close to high-income residential areasWorkers are skilled and well paid

Old factories near homes – found in old urban areas, e.g. Kwun Tong, San Po Kong, Cheung Sha Wan, Tsuen Wan, Kwai Chung.

  • 👍 Factory owners can find workers easily nearby.
  • 👍 Local residents can find jobs near home and save travel time and money.
  • 👎 Problems for residents: air pollution, noise pollution, threat to safety (e.g. fire) → answer D (all three).

Why did factories move out (mid-1970s to 1990s)?
Industrial areas spread from both sides of Victoria Harbour to new towns outside the urban centre. This is because land in the urban centre was limited and expensive. New towns had more and cheaper land.

High-tech areas: Hong Kong Science Park (Sha Tin / Tolo Harbour) and Cyberport (south-west Hong Kong Island).

  • Need lots of talent 人才
  • Pleasant environment
  • Make high-tech products
  • Developed since the late 1990s (not the early 1990s)

Squatter area example: small huts on a hillside / slope near a high-income area. Small in scale, and the environment is poor (crowded and dirty).

Map skill: Kwong Fuk Estate and Tai Po Industrial Estate

  • Kwong Fuk Estate = public housing estate = low-income area.
  • Industrial Estate X = Tai Po Industrial Estate.
  • Is Kwong Fuk Estate easily affected by pollution? No – it is not, because it is far away and the Waterfront Park separates them as a buffer.
Measuring distance: put a paper strip between the two places, mark both ends, then lay it on the scale bar and read the metres.
Reduced bearing 簡化方位角: start from N or S, then turn toward E or W. Science Park → Cyberport is about S 25° W. Always measure with your protractor on the quiz map.
