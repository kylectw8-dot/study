3.2 Why do landslides occur in Hong Kong?

Natural and human factors.

A landslide 山泥傾瀉 is the rapid movement of a mass of soil or rocks down a slope due to the pull of gravity 地心吸力.

Natural factors

  • 🌧️ Heavy rain (common in summer): the pores 孔隙 between soil particles fill with water. This reduces the strength / friction of slope materials, so the slope becomes unstable.
  • 🪨 Deep weathering 風化 makes thick layers of loose materials.
  • ⛰️ Hilly relief: about 60% of natural slopes in HK are steeper than 20°. Steeper slopes have a higher risk.

Weathering steps

  1. Rainwater seeps into the rocks.
  2. Joints in the rocks are widened.
  3. The rocks are broken down into smaller pieces.
  4. Thick layers of loose materials are formed.
Order: d → b → a → c

Human factors

  • Cut-and-fill slopes were developed to provide land for buildings and roads.
  • Many man-made slopes are steep with a larger slope angle than natural slopes.
  • Some man-made slopes were poorly constructed / maintained in the early years.

Why did man-made slopes cause many landslides in the early years?
They were steep (larger angle) and some were poorly constructed or maintained.

3.3 What are the negative impacts of landslides?

What happens when a slope fails.

  • 🚧 Disruption of road transport (roads blocked)
  • ⚡ Damage to electricity poles, water pipes, buildings
  • 💔 Loss of lives and injuries
Landslides do not trigger earthquakes or cause droughts.

1972 Po Shan Road landslide 寶珊道山泥傾瀉

  • On Hong Kong Island (Mid-Levels), not Kowloon.
  • One of the most fatal landslides in HK (67 deaths).
  • It caused the collapse of buildings (Kotewall Court).

3.4 How does Hong Kong prepare for and respond to landslides?

Engineering, planning and warnings.

Slope-stabilising engineering works

FunctionWorks
Provide physical supportSoil nails 泥釘, retaining walls 護土牆
Trap fallen slope materialsRigid barriers 剛性屏障, flexible barriers (wire nets) 柔性屏障
Improve drainageSurface drainage channels, horizontal drains 排水道
  • Covering slopes with chunam / shotcrete (hard surface) stops water seeping in.
  • Greening of slopes 斜坡綠化: planting covers the slope. It looks more natural and beautiful and roots help hold the soil.

Land use planning

  • Clear squatter areas on dangerous slopes.
  • Conduct landslide risk assessments before development.

Preventive measures (before a landslide)

  • Limit the use of slopes through legislation.
  • Educate the public, e.g. exhibitions about slope safety.
  • Issue a Landslip Warning 山泥傾瀉警告.
Opening temporary shelters and sending rescue teams happen after a landslide. They are responses, not preventive measures.
