4.1 What is the structure of Earth?

Three layers and seven plates.

LayerFacts
Crust 地殼Thinnest layer. Continental crust is thicker than oceanic crust.
Mantle 地幔Hot, partly melted rock. Cooler than the core. Has convection currents.
Core 地核Hottest. Outer core is liquid; inner core is solid.
Oceanic crust is thinner than continental crust. Do not mix them up.

Seven major plates 板塊

Eurasian, Indo-Australian, Pacific, North American, South American, African, Antarctic.

"Every Island Plays Nice Songs At All" → Eurasian, Indo-Australian, Pacific, North American, South American, African, Antarctic.
  • Hong Kong is on the Eurasian Plate 歐亞板塊, a mainly continental plate. The Pacific Plate is mainly oceanic.

4.2 How does plate movement lead to earthquakes?

Moving plates build up pressure.

  • Plate movement is driven by convection currents 對流 in the mantle.
MovementWhat happens
Divergent 分離Plates move apart ← →
Convergent 聚合Plates move towards each other → ←
Transform 錯動Plates slide past each other ↑↓

Parts of an earthquake

  • Focus 震源: the point underground where rocks break.
  • Epicentre 震央: the point on the surface directly above the focus.
  • Seismic waves 地震波: energy spreading out from the focus.
  • Fault 斷層: the crack where rocks move.

How an earthquake forms

  1. When plates move, pressure builds up along the plate boundary.
  2. The accumulated pressure exceeds the limits of the plates.
  3. Rocks break and move.
  4. The stored energy is released as seismic waves.
  5. The earth shakes: an earthquake.

Check yourself

Plate movement is driven by ______ currents in the mantle.
convection
Plates moving apart is ______; towards each other is ______; sliding past is ______.
divergent; convergent; transform
What is the focus? The epicentre?
Focus: where rocks break underground. Epicentre: the point on the surface directly above the focus.
Order the earthquake formation steps.
Pressure builds → exceeds limits → rocks break and move → energy released as seismic waves → earth shakes.

4.3 Distribution and impacts of earthquakes

Where they happen and what they do.

  • Most earthquakes occur along plate boundaries 板塊邊界.
  • Circum-Pacific Belt 環太平洋地震帶 (Pacific Ring of Fire): around the Pacific Ocean. About 80–90% of the world’s earthquakes happen here.
  • Alpine-Himalayan Belt 歐亞地震帶: stretches across Europe and Asia.
  • Magnitude is measured by the Richter scale 黎克特制. Bigger magnitude → more damage (buildings collapse, roads crack).
Primary effects 直接影響Secondary effects 間接影響
Immediate, direct damage from ground shakingDamage caused by the primary effects
Buildings collapse, roads and ground crack, bridges breakTsunamis 海嘯, fires, landslides, disease
A tsunami destroys coastal facilities and causes loss of lives, but it does not cause more earthquakes.
