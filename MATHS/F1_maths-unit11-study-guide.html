SHW 11-A1

11A Congruent triangles

Same shape, same size.

  • Congruent figures (≅) have the same shape and size. One can fit exactly on the other.
  • In △ABC ≅ △PQR, the order of letters tells you the matching parts: A↔P, B↔Q, C↔R.
  • Corresponding sides are equal [corr. sides, ≅△s]: AB = PQ, BC = QR, CA = RP.
  • Corresponding angles are equal [corr. ∠s, ≅△s]: ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R.
△ABC ≅ △PQR, AB = 6 cm → PQ = 6 cm [corr. sides, ≅△s]
Read the letters in order: 1st ↔ 1st, 2nd ↔ 2nd, 3rd ↔ 3rd. Don't trust the picture's position — it may be rotated or flipped.

SHW 11-B1

11B Conditions for congruent triangles

SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS.

ConditionWhat is equal
SSS3 pairs of sides
SAS2 sides and the included angle (the angle between them)
ASA2 angles and the included side
AAS2 angles and a non-included side
RHSRight angle, hypotenuse and one other side
AAA and SSA are NOT conditions for congruence.

Writing a proof

In △ABC and △ADC,
AB = AD  (given)
∠BAC = ∠DAC  (given)
AC = AC  (common side)
∴ △ABC ≅ △ADC  (SAS)
  • A side shared by both triangles is a common side.
  • Parallel lines give equal alternate angles [alt. ∠s, AD // BC].

Check yourself

No (same shape but maybe different size)
What does the H in RHS stand for?
Hypotenuse
SHW 11-C1

11C Using congruent triangles in proofs

Prove ≅ first, then use it.

  1. Prove the two triangles are congruent (with a condition).
  2. Then use corr. sides or corr. ∠s of ≅△s to prove sides or angles are equal.
  • C is the mid-point of BD → BC = DC.
  • △ABC ≅ △EDC (RHS) → AB = ED [corr. sides, ≅△s].
  • △ABC ≅ △ADC with C on BD: ∠ACB = ∠ACD and they add up to 180° → each is 90° → AC ⊥ BD; and BC = DC = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 cm.
The word "Hence" means use the result from part (a).
