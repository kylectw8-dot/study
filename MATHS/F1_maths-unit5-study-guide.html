5A Significant figures

Which digits count?

  • The 1st significant figure is the first non-zero digit from the left.
  • Leading zeros (in front) are not significant: 0.0642 has 3 s.f.
  • Zeros between non-zero digits are significant: 80102 has 5 s.f.
  • Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant: 0.03520 has 4 s.f.

Rounding off

Look at the next digit: 5 or more → round up; less than 5 → keep. Keep place-holding zeros!

  • 3197 ≈ 3000 (1 s.f.)
  • 8995 ≈ 9000 (2 s.f.)
  • 105.48 ≈ 105 (3 s.f.)
  • 24.7093 ≈ 24.71 (4 s.f.)
  • 0.008 14 ≈ 0.0081 (2 s.f.)
  • 310 875 ≈ 300 000 (1 s.f.), 310 000 (2 s.f.), 311 000 (3 s.f.)

5B Rounding up, rounding down and estimation

Always up or always down.

MethodRuleExample
Round offLook at next digit (≥ 5 up)23.45 ≈ 23 (nearest integer)
Round upAlways go up if anything is left2182 ≈ 2190 (nearest ten)
Round downAlways cut off (go down)0.0345 ≈ 0.034 (2 s.f.)
  • Round up: 8795 → 8800 (2 s.f.); 24.7093 → 24.8 (1 d.p.)
  • Round down: 230 051 → 230 000 (4 s.f.); 23.45 → 23 (nearest integer)

Estimation

7.2 + 2.6: round off → 7 + 3 = 10; round down → 7 + 2 = 9; round up → 8 + 3 = 11
580 ÷ 120: round off → 600 ÷ 100 = 6; down → 500 ÷ 100 = 5; up → 600 ÷ 200 = 3
Use round up when you must have enough (e.g. buses for students). Use round down when you cannot go over (e.g. how many items you can afford).
