12A Data and frequency tables

Counting and organising.

  • Discrete data can be counted (number of credit cards, number of students).
  • Continuous data are measured (weight, height, time).
  • A frequency distribution table uses tally marks (卌) and frequencies.
15 textbooks: 2 + 4 + x + 3 = 15 → x = 6
Below $200: 2 + 4 = 6 → 6/15 = 40%
$150 or above: 4 + 6 + 3 = 13

Bar charts

  • Each bar's height shows the frequency. Read the title and the axes first.
  • Total = sum of all bar heights.

12B Stem-and-leaf diagrams and pie charts

Reading the charts.

Stem-and-leaf diagram

  • Stem = tens, leaf = units: stem 12, leaf 5 → $125.
  • Count the leaves to find how many data there are.
  • Prices: stems 12 to 16 with 5, 6, 7, 4, 2 leaves → 24 shops. Highest = 16|7 = $167.
  • Below $140: 5 + 6 = 11 shops. $150 or above: 4 + 2 = 6 → 6/24 = 25%.
  • Leaves are in order, so in "0 | 3 x 5", x can be 3, 4 or 5.

Pie chart

  • The whole circle is 360°. Unknown angle: x = 360° − 84° − 126° − 36° = 114°.
  • Number in a sector = angle ÷ 360° × total. 84° of 60 students → 84/360 × 60 = 14.
