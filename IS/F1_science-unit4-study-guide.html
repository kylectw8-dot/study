4.1 Cells and the microscope

The basic unit of living things.

  • The basic unit of living things is the cell 細胞.
  • Unicellular 單細胞 organisms have one cell (e.g. Amoeba). Multicellular 多細胞 organisms have more than one cell.
  • In school we use a light microscope 光學顯微鏡. An electron microscope magnifies much more.

Parts of a light microscope

PartJob
Eyepiece 目鏡The lens you look through (at the top)
Objective 物鏡The lens near the slide (4X, 10X, 40X)
Coarse adjustment knob 粗調節器Big knob for large focusing movements
Fine adjustment knob 微調節器Small knob for small focusing movements
Stage, mirror / lightHolds the slide; gives light

Magnification

Total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification
  • Eyepiece 10X with objective 4X → 10 × 4 = 40X.
  • 20X = objective 4X × eyepiece 5X. 100X = 10X × 10X. 600X = 40X × 15X.
  • Longer eyepiece → lower magnification. Longer objective → higher magnification.
  • Higher magnification → smaller field of view (you see fewer cells, bigger).
  • The image is magnified 放大 and inverted 倒轉 (upside down and left–right swapped). The letter "d" looks like "p".
Eyepiece is opposite: a long eyepiece = low power. Objective is the same way round: long objective = high power.

4.2 Plant and animal cells

What is inside a cell?

StructureFunctionPlantAnimal
Cell membrane 細胞膜Controls what goes in and out✓✓
Cytoplasm 細胞質Where chemical reactions happen✓✓
Nucleus 細胞核Controls the cell; contains genetic materials✓✓
Cell wall 細胞壁Supports and protects the cell, gives shape✓✗
Chloroplast 葉綠體Green; carries out photosynthesis✓ (green parts)✗
Vacuole 液泡Stores cell sap✓ large✗ / small
A green leaf cell has a regular shape, cell wall and chloroplasts. A cheek (mouth) cell is irregular with no cell wall. Onion cells have a cell wall but no chloroplasts (not green).

Preparing an onion cell slide

  1. (C) Peel off a piece of skin from an onion scale leaf.
  2. (A) Spread it on a microscope slide.
  3. (D) Add a drop of iodine solution (stains the cells so the nucleus is easy to see).
  4. (E) Cover with a cover slip (lower it slowly to avoid air bubbles; stops cells drying out).
  5. (B) Soak up extra iodine with tissue paper.
Order: C → A → D → E → B

Drawing cells

  • Use a sharp pencil, clear single lines, no shading.
  • Label lines are straight, drawn with a ruler, and do not cross.
  • Add a title and magnification, e.g. "Onion cell (× 100)".

4.3 Human reproduction + 4.6 Heredity

Sex cells, puberty and passing on traits.

Sex cells 性細胞

Sperm 精子Egg (ovum) 卵子
Made byMale (testes)Female (ovaries)
SizeVery small (about 5 micrometres head)Large (about 100 micrometres)
ShapeTadpole shape with a tailRound
MovementCan swim by itselfCannot move by itself
Cell A (sperm) is smaller than cell B (egg). Cell A has a tail but cell B is round.

Puberty 青春期

  • During puberty there is more sex hormones 性激素 in the body. Secondary sexual characteristics 第二性徵 appear.
  • Boys: beard grows, shoulders widen, larynx gets larger (deeper voice).
  • Girls: breasts develop, hips widen.
Production of semen is not a secondary sexual characteristic. It is a reproductive function.

Heredity 遺傳

  • Passing on traits from one generation to the next is heredity. It happens because genetic information in DNA is passed on.
  • A human body cell has 46 chromosomes 染色體. A sex cell has 23.
  • All living things are made up of cells. Cells are 3-D and come in many shapes.
