1.1 Learning about Science

What is science? Discovery or invention?

  • Through studying science 科學, we understand how the natural world works.
  • Science is based on evidence 證據. Doing experiments 實驗 is one way to collect evidence.
  • Scientific knowledge is subject to change when new evidence is found.
  • If scientific inventions are misused, they can harm us.
Discovery 發現Invention 發明
MeaningFinding something that already exists in natureMaking something new that did not exist
ExampleMarie Curie found radium and polonium (1898)Thomas Edison's light bulb (1879)
Discovery = uncovering what is already there. Invention = invent something new.

Qualities of a good scientist (like Marie Curie): hardworking, cares for others, does not give up easily.

evidence

1.2 Practice of science and fair tests

How do scientists investigate?

Steps in a scientific investigation 科學探究

  1. Making observations 觀察
  2. Asking a question
  3. Proposing a hypothesis 假說
  4. Carrying out experiments
  5. Drawing a conclusion 結論
"Only Quiet Hamsters Eat Cheese" → Observe, Question, Hypothesis, Experiment, Conclusion.

Three types of variables

VariableWhat we doMemory hook
Independent variable 自變量We change it (only ONE)"I change it"
Dependent variable 因變量We measure itIt depends on the change
Controlled variables 控制變量We keep the sameWe control them
Look for the words in the question. "different mass" → mass is the independent variable. "time taken" → time is the dependent variable.

Fair test sentence patterns (bonus mark!)

✅ It is a fair test because there is only one variable changed, which is the (type of flame).
❌ It is not a fair test because there is more than one variable changed. The (volume of water) should be kept the same.

Check yourself

Objects of different mass fall from a fixed height. Find IV and DV.
IV: mass of the object. DV: time taken to fall to the ground.
Angle of an inclined plane and time for a ball to roll down. Find IV and DV.
IV: angle of the inclined plane. DV: time for the ball to reach the ground.
Density of water at different temperatures. Find IV and DV.
IV: temperature of water. DV: density of water.
Blue vs yellow flame, Group A: same beakers, same water level. Fair test?
Yes. It is a fair test because there is only one variable changed, which is the type of flame.
Group B: one beaker has more water. Fair test?
No. It is not a fair test because there is more than one variable changed. The volume of water should be kept the same.
Washing powder (Jamie): list IV, controlled variables, DV.
IV: brand of washing powder. Controlled: mass of powder (20 g), volume of water (5 L), type of cloth, water temperature. DV: time taken to remove the stain.
Is Jamie's conclusion reasonable?
No. It is not a fair test. The stains are different sizes (the stain in the Super Clean tub is bigger). The size of the stain should be kept the same.
Luke's cups (metal, glass, plastic): IV? Fair test?
IV: material of the cup. Not a fair test: the shape of the cup and the volume of water also changed.

1.3 Safety in the laboratory

Hazard labels, fire and safe work.

We usually do experiments in the laboratory 實驗室. Hazard warning labels 危險警告標記 on reagent bottles warn us about dangerous chemicals.

Picture on labelMeaning
☠️ Skull and crossbonesToxic 有毒
🔥 FlameFlammable 易燃
💧✋ Liquid dripping on a hand and a surfaceCorrosive 腐蝕性
💥 Exploding objectExplosive 爆炸性
The dripping-on-hand symbol is corrosive, not flammable. This was a true/false question in the mock test.

The fire triangle 火三角

🪵 Fuel💨 Oxygen🌡️ High temperature

To put out a fire, remove at least one part of the fire triangle.

Wildfire methodWhich part is removed?
Backfiring (burning in front of the fire)Removes the fuel
WaterCools the fire and removes the high temperature
FoamForms a blanket that cuts off oxygen

Fire-fighting equipment: fire extinguisher 滅火筒, fire blanket 滅火氈, sand bucket 滅火沙桶.
Not for fires: eye wash bottle 洗眼瓶, first aid box 急救箱.

Mary's experiment

  • 👍 Good: she wears safety spectacles, uses a test tube holder, and her hair is tied up.
  • 👎 Mistake: the flammable chemical X is placed near the flame. It may catch fire.

Check yourself

Name the three parts of the fire triangle.
Fuel, oxygen, high temperature
How does backfiring stop a wildfire?
By removing the fuel in front of the wildfire.
Why is foam used on fires?
It forms a blanket over surfaces and cuts off the oxygen supply.
Which can put out a small lab fire: fire blanket, eye wash, sand bucket, first aid box?
Fire blanket and sand bucket (mock test answer B)

Labelled sectional diagram (heating water + measuring temperature)

How to get full marks
  • Use a pencil and ruler. No wavy lines.
  • Draw water in the beaker with a straight line.
  • The thermometer bulb is in the water, not touching the bottom.
  • Keep sizes sensible (beaker smaller than tripod top).
  • Label all 7: thermometer, beaker, water, wire gauze, tripod, Bunsen burner, insulating mat.
  • Label lines are straight and do not cross.
"Sectional" = a side view cut in half. Draw the beaker as 3 sides of a box (open top). For a conical flask, draw a triangle with a neck.

What is the use of a wire gauze?
We can use a wire gauze to support a beaker and spread the heat evenly.
What is the use of a dropper?
We can use a dropper to transfer a small amount of liquid.
What is the use of a glass rod?
We can use a glass rod to stir a liquid.
True or false: a dropper should be held upside down after it is filled.
False. Liquid would run into the rubber bulb.
Order to light a Bunsen burner: (1) light match, (2) open air hole, (3) turn on gas, (4) close air hole
(4) → (1) → (3) → (2). Close the air hole, light the match, turn on the gas, then open the air hole.

Luminous flame 光焰: yellow, air hole closed, sooty, not very hot. Safe "resting" flame.

Non-luminous flame 無光焰: blue, air hole open, much hotter. Use it for heating.

1.4B Basic practical skills: measuring

Which instrument? Which unit?

We can measure the (quantity) with a/an (instrument) in (unit).
QuantityInstrumentUnit
TemperatureThermometer 溫度計degrees Celsius (°C)
Volume of a liquidMeasuring cylinder 量筒cm³ or mL
MassElectronic balance 電子天平 / triple beam balancegrams (g)
Curved length (circumference)Measuring tape 軟尺cm or mm
TimeStopwatch 秒錶seconds (s)
Straight lengthMetre rule 米尺 / rulercm, m
Always write the unit. No unit = lose a mark. 1 mL = 1 cm³.

Reading a measuring cylinder

  • Put your eye level with the liquid surface (not above, not below).
  • Read the bottom of the meniscus 彎液面 (the curved surface).
  • Count what one small mark is worth first. Example: 2 big numbers 28 → 30 with 10 small gaps → each gap = 0.2 mL.

Reading a thermometer

  • Keep the bulb in the liquid while reading.
  • Wait until the reading stops changing.
  • Give the answer to 1 decimal place, e.g. 36.4 °C.

Volume by displacement (objects that sink or float)

  1. Read the water level first.
  2. Put the object in. Read the new level.
  3. Volume of object = new reading − first reading

For a floating cork: tie it to plasticine so it sinks fully.
Volume of cork = (water + plasticine + cork) − (water + plasticine).

Repeat the measurement 3 times and take the average to make the result more accurate (reduce errors).

Check yourself

Mock test: water only = 42 mL, water + plasticine = 44 mL. Volume of plasticine?
44 − 42 = 2 mL (show the subtraction, write the unit)
Why tie the cork to plasticine?
The cork floats. The plasticine makes it sink completely under the water.
Sam takes the thermometer out after a few seconds. Give two suggestions.
1. Wait until the reading is steady before reading. 2. Read it while the bulb is still in the water.
Karen uses the same dropper for liquid A and B. What is wrong?
Mistake: she did not wash the dropper (or use a clean one). Problem: liquid A gets into the bottle of liquid B and contaminates it.
Which student reads a ruler correctly: X (from left), Y (straight above), Z (from right)?
Y only. Eye directly above the mark.
How do you measure the circumference of a beaker?
We can measure the circumference of a beaker with a measuring tape in centimetres (cm).
How do you measure a 100 m runner's time?
We can measure the time with a stopwatch in seconds (s).
