6.1 Matter and particle theory

Everything is made of tiny particles.

Matter 物質 is anything that has mass and takes up space.

The particle theory 粒子理論

  1. All matter is made up of particles.
  2. The particles are tiny.
  3. The particles are moving randomly all the time.
  4. There are empty spaces between the particles.
  5. Different substances are made up of different particles.
  • Silver is made up of silver atoms 原子.
  • Oxygen is made up of oxygen molecules 分子. Each molecule has two oxygen atoms.

Smelling bread from outside a bakery

The bread is made up of particles. Some of them escape into the air. These particles move randomly in the spaces between air particles, so we can smell bread at a distance.

Mixing alcohol and water

  • 300 cm³ alcohol + 100 cm³ water: alcohol % = 300 ÷ 400 × 100% = 75%.
  • The total volume is less than 400 cm³ because water and alcohol are made of different particles of different sizes. Smaller particles fit into the spaces between the larger ones.
Draw the mixture as big "A" circles with small "W" circles in the gaps between them.

6.2 Particle model + 6.3 Dissolving

Solids, liquids and gases as particles.

SolidLiquidGas
ShapeFixedNot fixedNot fixed
VolumeFixedFixedNot fixed
ArrangementClosely packed in a regular patternClosely packed, no regular patternFar apart
MovementVibrate about fixed positionsMove around each otherMove freely and randomly in all directions, fast
Similarity: Both solids and liquids have a fixed volume.
Difference: Solids have a fixed shape but liquids do not.
  • Ice vs water: particles in ice are in a regular pattern but particles in water are not. Particles in ice vibrate about fixed positions but particles in water move around.
  • Brown gas spreads into an empty flask: gases do not have a fixed volume or shape; gas particles can move in all directions. Draw particles evenly spread in both containers.

Dissolving as particles

  • Solute particles move apart and mix with the solvent particles. They become evenly distributed.
  • Total mass is conserved (stays the same): 5 g salt + 50 g water = 55 g. The total volume may decrease.

6.4 Thermal expansion and contraction

Things get bigger when hot and smaller when cold.

  • When heated, particles gain kinetic energy and move faster. The spaces between particles increase, so the substance expands 膨脹.
  • When cooled, spaces decrease, so it contracts 收縮.
The particles themselves do not get bigger and their number does not change. Only the spaces between them change.
  • Gases expand to a greater extent than solids and liquids because gas particles can move much farther apart.

Cause-and-effect sentences

[effect] because [cause]: The gas expands because the spaces between particles increase when the temperature is high.
[cause] so [effect]: When the temperature is higher, the particles gain more energy so they move faster.

Examples

  • The Eiffel Tower is 15 cm taller in summer: the spaces between iron particles increase.
  • Tight jar lid: pour hot water on the lid so the lid expands (metal expands more than glass).
  • Coloured water in a tube: in 80 °C water it expands so the level rises; in ice water it contracts so the level drops. The 25 °C set-up is the control for comparison.
  • Variables: IV = temperature of water in the beaker. Controlled = time, initial water level, length of glass tube, volume of coloured water, position of the boiling tube.

Ball and ring (same metal)

CasePass?Why
Only the ball is heated❌ CannotThe ball expands but the ring does not.
Only the ring is heated✅ CanThe ring expands, so the hole is bigger. The ball is smaller than the hole.
Both heated the same✅ CanBoth expand by the same extent.
  • Railways bend in summer → leave gaps between parts of the railway.
  • Power cables may break in winter → use longer (loose) cables.
